Primary: Class 1 to Class 5

The years when reading, writing and arithmetic become secure, and a child starts to work independently.

What these five years are for

Almost everything a child does later rests on what happens here. A child who leaves Class 5 reading fluently and confident with numbers can manage the middle school years. A child who does not will struggle regardless of how good the later teaching is.

So the priority through Primary is straightforward: read well, write clearly, and handle arithmetic without fear.

Subjects

Subjects taught in the primary classes
Subject What children do
English Reading aloud and silently, handwriting, spelling, grammar, and writing sentences and then paragraphs of their own
Kannada Reading and writing in Kannada, taught as a language subject throughout
Mathematics Number work, the four operations, tables, fractions, measurement, money, time and simple geometry
Environmental Studies / Science The body, plants and animals, weather, materials, and the first simple experiments
Social Studies Family and neighbourhood, the village and district, Karnataka, and the beginnings of history and geography
Computers Using a computer confidently: keyboard and mouse, drawing and typing, and simple tasks in the computer room
Physical education, art and music Games and exercise, drawing and craft, songs and rhythm
To be confirmed

The exact subject list and weekly periods per subject, the class in which Hindi is introduced, and the textbooks used.

Homework

Homework in these classes should be short and finishable by the child alone. It is there to practise something already taught, not to teach something new, and not to occupy the evening. If your child is regularly unable to finish, that is worth telling the class teacher, because it usually means something was not understood in class.

To be confirmed

Expected homework time per class, and the school's homework policy.

How progress is checked

Teachers assess through the year in class as well as through formal examinations, following the state board pattern. The point of assessment at this stage is to catch a child who is falling behind while it is still easy to fix.

To be supplied

Examination schedule, report card timing, parent-teacher meeting dates, and what support is offered to a child who is behind in reading or mathematics.
