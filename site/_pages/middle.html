Middle: Class 6 to Class 8

Subjects separate out, science moves into the laboratory, and children start taking responsibility for their own work.

What changes in Class 6

Up to Class 5 a child mostly has one teacher. From Class 6 there is a different teacher for each subject, more books, more written work, and an expectation that the student keeps track of their own timetable and deadlines.

Some children find the first term unsettling. It usually passes by the second term. What helps is a fixed study time at home and a check that the day's work has actually been written down.

Subjects

  • English — literature and language, longer written work, comprehension
  • Kannada and Hindi as language subjects
  • Mathematics — algebra begins, along with geometry, ratio and percentages
  • Science — physics, chemistry and biology topics, with laboratory work
  • Social Science — history, geography, civics and economics
  • Computer Science — regular timetabled sessions in the computer room
  • Physical education, art and music

Science in the laboratory

From Class 6 science stops being only a textbook subject. Students handle apparatus, run experiments themselves, record what they observe, and write it up. Watching a teacher demonstrate is not the same thing, and the difference shows later in Class 10 practicals.

What is actually available in the laboratories is listed on the science and technology page.

Projects

Middle school is where students take on work that runs over several weeks rather than a single evening: a science fair entry, a social science investigation of their own village, a model built and explained. This is the point at which some students discover a subject they care about.

Sports, clubs and school events matter more at this age than at any other, and they are often what makes a student want to come in. See student life.
