Science and technology

"A School with Science & Technology" is on the school crest. This page is where that claim has to be backed with specifics.

Why this page matters more than most

Science and technology is the school's stated identity, so this is the page a sceptical parent will read closely and then check against what they see on a visit. It needs real equipment lists and real photographs of students using them. Where the substance is not there yet, it is better to say less than to inflate — the gap between the page and the visit is worse than a modest page.

Science laboratory

Practical work runs from Class 6 upward, building towards the Class 10 SSLC practicals. Students handle the apparatus themselves, record what they observe and write it up, rather than watching a demonstration from a desk.

To be supplied

Whether physics, chemistry and biology have separate laboratories or share one room; the number of student work stations; the main apparatus available in each subject; safety equipment; and how many practical periods each class gets per week.

Computer room

Children use computers from the primary classes onward. The measure that matters is how many children share a machine in a session — two to a computer is workable, six is not.

To be supplied

Number of computers, students per computer in a session, whether there is an internet connection, what software is used, and how many computer periods each class gets.

Science fair and projects

Middle and secondary students build and present projects of their own. This is often where a student first finds a subject they want to pursue.

To be supplied

Whether the school runs its own science fair and when; projects students have built; participation in taluk, district or state level science competitions; and any prizes won. Photographs of students with their projects would carry this page.
