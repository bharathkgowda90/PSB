Secondary: Class 9 and Class 10

Two years of steady preparation for the Karnataka SSLC board examination, and the decisions that follow it.

The SSLC examination

At the end of Class 10 students sit the Karnataka Secondary School Leaving Certificate examination, set by the Karnataka School Examination and Assessment Board. It is the first public examination most students take, and the marks affect which PUC course and college a student can go on to.

Preparation is spread across both years rather than crammed into the final months. Class 9 matters: the syllabus builds directly into Class 10, and a student who is weak in Class 9 mathematics or science spends Class 10 catching up instead of revising.

Subjects

  • First, second and third languages, following the state board's three-language pattern
  • Mathematics
  • Science — physics, chemistry and biology, including practical work
  • Social Science — history, geography, political science and economics

How students are prepared

  • Syllabus completed with time left for revision, not finished in the final week
  • Regular unit tests through both years so weak areas surface early
  • Preparatory examinations written under real board conditions and timings
  • Practice with previous years' question papers
  • Laboratory practicals taken seriously, since they carry marks
  • Extra coaching for students who need it, and for those aiming at high marks

After Class 10

SSLC is not the end of the road, and families often have to decide quickly once results are out. The usual options are:

  • PUC (Class 11 and 12) in Science, Commerce or Arts, at a pre-university college
  • Diploma courses in polytechnics, entered directly after Class 10
  • ITI and vocational training for trade qualifications

Teachers here talk to students about these choices before results day rather than after, so that families are not deciding under time pressure.
